## Play

**Double click the standalone game's index.html. You're ready to cook.**

The whole game lives in that one file and plays offline in your browser. Nothing to install, no Flash plugin and no server to start. The ready to play file is included in this repository at **[dist/standalone/index.html](dist/standalone/index.html)**. Download the repository, extract it and open that file.

Click **Start**, then follow the instructions in the game. Select the batter, pour onto an empty griddle spot, flip when ready, and plate your dosas before serving the waiting customers. Keep an eye on their patience!

Choose **Classic** for the original effects or **Optimized** for lighter effects and helpful cooking cues. You can switch above the game without restarting your day. Optimized also includes the fullscreen button. If the radio is quiet, click inside the game to enable sound.

## The food behind the game

Those little pancakes are **dosas**, or **dosai** in Tamil. Rice and urad dal batter is fermented, spread on a hot griddle called a **tawa**, and cooked into a soft pancake or a crisp crêpe. Chutney and sambar are familiar companions. [About dosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dosa_%28food%29).

A **dhaba** is an informal roadside restaurant: a place for a meal, tea and a break from the journey. **Madrasi** suggests Madras, now Chennai. Together with the warm light, passing traffic and busy cook, the name invites an image of people gathering for an evening meal. [About dhabas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhaba).

Chennai's **Dosa Mama** offers a lovely real life parallel: a neighbourhood cook feeding regulars and families in the evening. [Read his story](https://www.dtnext.in/city/2020/02/05/for-dosa-mama-neighbourhood-is-family-he-cooks-for-them) · [See an evening dosa stall](https://venkatarangan.com/blog/2019/03/homely-dosa-varieties/).

*These connections are guesses and reading notes, not an independently fact checked history of the game. No particular restaurant has been established as its inspiration.*

## What's on the radio?

Two old cinema songs are the current candidates for the game's music. The exact recordings still need confirmation, but their words offer two very different moods.

**Naan Anaiyittaal · Tamil · T. M. Soundararajan**

The title roughly means **“If I give the order.”** The song imagines leadership that protects people from poverty and injustice. It values dignity and responsibility, with an emphatic, hopeful tone. It comes from *Enga Veettu Pillai*, associated with M. G. Ramachandran. [Listen to the game recording](assets/sound/162-d812a4f894dc.mp3).

**Jhanan Jhanwa More Bichwa · Hindi · Geeta Dutt**

This candidate from *Mangala* has a playful, affectionate mood. Its lyrics draw on Radha and Krishna, using jingling ornaments, a flute and butter as images of movement, attraction and longing. The beloved is addressed with tenderness and teasing. [Listen to the game recording](assets/sound/477-fa91518df41c.mp3).

One song speaks about public ideals; the other about affection and devotion. Hearing both at a roadside stall could evoke someone's favourite old records accompanying the working day. That is an interpretation, not a clue that locates the restaurant.

**[Read the lyrics' meaning, imagery and credits](docs/name-and-setting.md#meaning-in-english)** right here in the repository. The explanations are in English, with sources and translation limits alongside them.
